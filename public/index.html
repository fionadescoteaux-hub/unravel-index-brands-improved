The three rules that make this a diagnostic

1 · Candidate before conclusion. The lowest-scoring domain, or genuine tie, is the starting constraint candidate. It is not presented as validated until materiality, actionability and outcome evidence support it.

2 · Confidence is reported, never applied. A high score flagged as estimated is marked in the report as needing verification. The score is not adjusted — capping it would mean an honest respondent scored lower than an optimistic one.

3 · The gap is a finding. Your stated biggest challenge is captured but never scored. Where it differs from the diagnosed constraint, that divergence is usually the most useful line in the report.

How to read this

Each domain is scored from its two threshold-anchored answers and converted to a percentage. Those nine scores are weighted into a single index between 20 and 100. The lowest-scoring domain or tied domains become constraint candidates — not automatically validated constraints.

Domains scored highly but flagged as estimated rather than evidenced are listed for verification against a royalty statement, a system export or a tracking study. Their scores are not adjusted, and the flag travels with the result.

The scale runs from 20 to 100 by construction, not 0 to 100. Answering at level one on every question scores 20. Twenty is the floor, not a near miss.
