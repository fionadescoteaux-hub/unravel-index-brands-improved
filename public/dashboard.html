Method

The Unravel Index applied to brand licensing. The nine domains and their weights are the canonical set, carried over unchanged from the purpose-led instrument — only the question language differs.

The nine domains

Weights sum to 100. Four domains are primary — they carry revenue directly. Five are enablers — they set the ceiling on what the primaries can achieve.

The instrument itself

Every question, and the observable measure that has to be true at each level. Nothing here is an adjective — a disputed score is settled by looking at something. Open a domain to read its two questions.

How scoring works

From answers to a constraintTwo threshold-anchored questions per domain, answered 1–5. The pair is averaged, converted to a percentage and multiplied by the domain weight. The weighted scores sum to an index between 20 and 100 for a complete assessment.
From candidate to validated constraintThe lowest-scoring domain or genuine tie is the starting candidate, not a causal conclusion. It advances to priority when commercially material and actionable, and to validated only when outcome evidence supports the intervention.
Maturity bandsFragile 20–39 · Emerging 40–59 · Defined 60–74 · Embedded 75–89 · Optimised 90–100. Scores below 20 cannot be produced by a complete assessment. Bands describe the commercial engine, not the brand’s desirability.

What the instrument will not do

Confidence is reported, never appliedA domain scored highly but flagged as estimated is listed for verification. The score is untouched — capping it would mean an honest respondent scored lower than an optimistic one.
Scoring is server-sideThe browser posts raw answers only. No respondent sees a running score while answering, so answers are not tuned toward a number.
Not a valuationIt names the leading constraint candidate for each brand today. It does not say what the brand is worth, or what the category will do next.

Income at risk

Brands where half or more of income sits on one licensee or category. Here the constraint is not only a ceiling — it is downside if that relationship moves. These go to the front of the queue regardless of index points.

Leakage exposure

Brands reporting no royalty audit in two years, or income that is almost entirely guaranteed minimums. Unverified self-reporting is the most direct money left on the table; guarantee-only income means the renewal is the only lever.

How to read this audit

What the Index measures. Nine commercial domains, each scored 20–100 from two threshold-anchored questions, weighted into one index. The number that governs a brand is its lowest domain, not its average.

Constraint candidate. The lowest-scoring domain is a candidate — a hypothesis. It becomes a priority once it is placed against named commercial lines and a move is committed with an owner, and validated only when the recorded outcome is met with written evidence.

Severity is not confidence. Severity says how far the weakest capability sits below the evidenced level. Evidence confidence says how much of that reading rests on judgement rather than documents. A severe finding can be low-confidence — the audit says both.

Brand Audit vs Commercial Map. The audit diagnoses the engine (nine domains). The map lists the products, services and channels the diagnosis lands on. The Index never becomes a product catalogue.

Where the evidence lives. The Evidence tab holds the three tests, the document list, every question and answer, and why the current status was assigned.
