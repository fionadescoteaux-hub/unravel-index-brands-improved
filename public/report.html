How this report is built

Each domain is scored from two threshold-anchored questions, where every answer level is tied to an observable measure rather than an adjective. The nine domain scores are weighted into a single index between 20 and 100, which maps to one of five maturity bands.

The lowest-scoring domain is a constraint candidate, not causal proof. Weight does not choose the candidate. Materiality, a complete intervention and post-intervention commercial evidence advance it from candidate to priority to validated.

The scale runs from 20 to 100 by construction, not 0 to 100. A subject answering at level one on every question scores 20. Twenty is the floor, not a near miss — worth knowing before reading any single score as a percentage of what is possible.

Holding up means an answer reached level four or five — evidenced or tracked. Needs to change means it sat at three or below, and what is shown is the next level up: not advice, but the next observable state the instrument recognises.

Confidence is captured per domain and reported, never applied to the score. Where a respondent scores a domain highly but flags the answer as estimated rather than evidenced, the domain is listed for verification and the flag travels with the result. On this illustrative brand every domain was flagged as evidenced, so nothing is held.

Scoring runs on the server after submission. No respondent sees a running score while answering.
